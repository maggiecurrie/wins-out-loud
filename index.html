Why I built this
"When I started managing a team, I realized the best people were often the quietest about their wins. So we started saying them out loud."

When I started my freelance business, I hired a small team, and suddenly I was the one running the 1:1s. I kept noticing the same thing: people did great work all week, then showed up to our check-ins and couldn't remember half of it.

So we built a habit. Everyone jotted down wins as they happened, with what changed because of it and the moment behind it. Every week, those notes became a quick update. Our 1:1s got shorter and better, I could actually see what everyone was doing, and when it came time to give feedback or write a reference, the receipts were already there.

Wins Out Loud is that habit, turned into a tool anyone can use.

Not sure what counts?

Wins come in five shapes.

Shipped

Something launched, published, delivered, or finished.

Moved a number

Views, revenue, time saved, response rates. Small numbers count.

Fixed a problem

A mistake caught early. A crisis calmed. The fire that never started.

Helped someone

A teammate unblocked, a new hire trained, a doc everyone reused.

Grew

A new skill, a first time doing something, feedback you acted on.

A simple test: if you'd be a little annoyed that someone else got the credit, it's a win. Write it down.

For managers

Run better 1:1s. See your team's work.

This started as a team habit. Here's how to use it with yours.

One page, two people

Your report shares their 1:1 page before you meet. You see wins, work in progress, and blockers at a glance, then add notes and next steps together.

Spend the time on what matters

No more "so, what did you work on?" Skip the recap and go straight to blockers, growth, and decisions.

Celebrate the quiet wins

The "helped someone" and "fixed a problem" wins are easy to miss. Make a habit of naming them in team meetings.

Write better reviews and references

When review season comes, you both have months of specific, dated wins to work from, with proof.
